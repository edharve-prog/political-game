# Review: a 30-turn Claude game (2026-10-03)

This is the live review called for by Project 6 ("selected outcomes look sensible on manual
review of 30 turns") and by Project 13's SD-1 to SD-3 done-when. It ran once all the P1
scenario and response stories had merged.

## How it was run

- **Build:** main @ e3b7564 (after PR #34). This includes Project 17's engine fixes and the
  LLM call log.
- **Model access:** the default `claude-code` provider, `ModelConfig()` and
  `ForecastConfig()` defaults (4 candidates, judge on, sampled selection).
- **Game:** `GameConfig(seed=7, election_turn=30)`, toy UK world, knowledge recall on.
- **Player:** a scripted player rotating five styles:
  - option 1;
  - the last option;
  - "consult unions and business, phase it in, frame it as fairness";
  - option 1 plus "Also: act on <first in-tray item>";
  - a middle option "announced immediately with no consultation".
- **Cost:** 30 turns, 131 calls (about 4.4 per turn), roughly 90 s per turn. 11 calls (8%)
  were validation retries.
- **Replay:** `replay()` reproduced the final state exactly.

## What worked

- **Narrative quality is high.** Outcomes follow from the response. They quote the player's
  framing ("fairness for working families"), react to consultation versus imposition, and
  mention in-tray items in passing.
  - Example, T27: "Ministers had consulted both sides, and the phased renewals cap went
    through with little noise."
- **Delivery (RB-4) lands.** On turns with an imposed delivery, rebellion, strike and
  backlash candidates appeared and were often picked. Consulted turns produced the
  "talks buy time" outcomes.
- **Variety and duplicates are fine.** Six categories led, no title repeated, and the in-tray
  was always full.
- **Limits work as designed.** Diminishing returns and the feasibility checks fired with
  clear notes.

## Findings

### 1. Engine consistency alone picks the outcome (P1, EB-13)
- **What happened.** The consistency score is the geometric mean of `exp(-z²/2)`. Since EC-8
  counts omitted indicators as claims of zero, typical scores run from 1.0 down to 1e-25.
  Even with a weight of 0.4 and the 1e-6 floor, that swamps the judge and the base rate.
- **Example, T0.** The rosy "Decisive cap wins renters" outcome had a judge score of 0.20 and
  a consistency of 1.0, and got probability 0.98. Three plausible rivals (a judge score of
  0.45 for the best of them) got 0.01 or less.
- **How often.** In 13 of 30 turns at least one candidate had a probability under 0.01. In 9
  turns a candidate the judge rated 0.15 or more got under 0.03.
- **Effect.** The ensemble is mostly "whichever story copied the engine's numbers". Sampling
  versus argmax barely matters when one candidate has 0.98.
- **Proposed fix.** Temper consistency:
  - clip each claim's z (for example at 3);
  - or score with a heavier-tailed fit;
  - or raise the minimum spread.
- **Target.** No candidate with a judge score of 0.15 or more falls below a probability of
  0.03 because of consistency alone.
- This is EB-13's "revisit the consistency minimum spread", promoted to P1.

### 2. Storylines never let go (P1, new SD-9)
- **What happened.** Only 6 distinct lead storylines appeared in 30 turns.
  - The Chinese grid takeover storyline was on the agenda in all 30 turns: it led 11 and sat
    in the in-tray for 19. It reached stage 14 and never resolved.
  - The renters and Bank of England storylines did the same at stages 10 and 9.
- **Why.** The writer is shown every open storyline and keeps continuing them. In-tray items
  carry over and escalate. Nothing ages a storyline out.
- **Effect.** The game felt like three long sagas rather than a premiership.
- **Proposed fix:**
  - a storyline that reaches stage 6, or is 10 turns old, must come to a head (the writer is
    told this is its final stage, and its candidates must include a resolving outcome);
  - at most one in-tray item may continue an open storyline;
  - a new lead storyline must open at least every third turn.
- **Target.** In a 30-turn game, 10 or more distinct lead storylines, and none on the agenda
  in more than 40% of turns.

### 3. The Commons locks the player out for half the game (P1, new EB-14)
- **What happened.** Legislature support fell from 0.65 to below 0.5 by T13. It then stayed
  at 0.30–0.47 for the rest of the game, because backbench rebellions take large steps down
  and recovery is about 0.007 per turn.
- **Effect.** From T15, every `spend`, `tax` and `legislate` was blocked. Twelve turns had
  actions removed, and five (T15, T16, T23, T25, T28) played with no action at all.
- **Proposed fix:**
  - support recovers toward its baseline on a half-life of a few turns;
  - the player gets a way to rebuild it (whipping, concessions to backbenchers,
    `communicate` aimed at the legislature);
  - a blocked measure can go through at a political cost instead of vanishing.
- **Target.** With the scripted player, fewer than 10% of turns have every action blocked.

### 4. Outcomes describe measures the Commons blocked (P1, new RB-8)
- **What happened.** The forecaster only sees the actions that survived the limits, plus the
  delivery. It is not told what was blocked.
- **Example, T18.** The pay freeze and tax rise were both blocked, and the only action left
  was a statement to the Bank. The chosen outcome still read "Pay freeze sparks public
  sector walkouts".
- **Proposed fix.** Pass the limit notes to the outcome prompt as "Blocked: …". The
  narrative should then describe the defeat, which is a good story beat in itself.
- **Target.** No chosen narrative claims a blocked measure took effect.

### 5. Avoidable retries (P2, new RB-9)
11 of 131 calls were retries:
- 6 candidate sets used bare group ids (`business`, not `group:business`);
- 4 interpretations returned a clarifying question alongside actions;
- 1 scenario continued a closed storyline.

Mapping a bare id to its unique prefixed id, and dropping a clarifying question when
actions are present, would remove about 10 retries per 30 turns. Separately, one LLM
interpretation (T13) mixed `do_nothing` with a real action. It should be dropped as the
keyword interpreter already does.

### 6. The world barely moves (observation, for Project 11 and EB-11)
Over 30 turns:
- group approvals moved by at most 0.05 (pensioners 0.55 to 0.505);
- inflation went from 3.5 to 3.41 and the deficit from 4.5 to 4.75.

The election gave 46.6% of the vote and 259 seats. Outcomes read as dramatic but leave
little trace, which matches EB-11 (voters adapt) and needs calibration in Project 11.
There is no story here yet; it is recorded so Project 11 starts from it.
