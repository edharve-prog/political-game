# Political Game review: errors and enhancements

Oct 13, 2026 · @Ed

## Summary

Main at f09b02d (after PR #96) fixes every reproduction from the 12 October review, but the game now has a dominant strategy: four turns of free laws and speeches, one group a turn, win 5 of 5 offline games with 54.8% of the vote, against 47.7% for doing nothing.

The review found 24 errors (7 P1, 11 P2, 6 P3), 7 reasoning and modelling weaknesses, and 20 enhancements. Three themes carry most of the P1s:

- **Costless levers.** Duration is free, and laws, speeches, deregulation and spending aimed at "the country" have no fiscal or political downside beyond one turn's capital.
- **Double mean reversion.** The engine fades institution support and resolve settles it again, so whips backfire after they end and rebellions overshoot.
- **Offline reading.** Keyword matching misreads ordinary phrasing, including the Budget's own options, and can sack a minister by accident.

What was checked:

- All of `src/hog_sim` (about 11,300 lines), `ENGINE_RULES.md`, the backlogs and both earlier reviews.
- The test suite: 519 passed, 5 skipped, in 4 min 33 s. Ruff was not run: PyPI is blocked from this workspace.
- Every finding marked "reproduced" was run as a probe against the toy UK world, seed 1 unless stated. No model calls were made and no repository files were changed.

Priority follows the last review: P1 = fix before trusting results, P2 = important consistency or robustness fix, P3 = lower impact.

## Errors A: exploits and balance

Levers that cost nothing let a player buy a majority early and coast; the balance test misses it because every bot plays one-turn actions.

One action on turn 0 at full size, then nothing, quiet scenarios and no outcome effects, 24 turns. Doing nothing ends at 48.0% of the vote, 287 seats.

### E1 Duration is free (P1, reproduced)

**Cause.** `capital_cost()` in `policy/limits.py` prices `abs(magnitude)` only, and `duration_turns` has no upper bound (`core/models.py`, and the review editor accepts any `N turns`). Kinds with no fiscal cost (legislate, communicate, deregulate, appoint, regulate) pay nothing for each extra turn. Deregulating the UK for 1 turn or 24 costs the same 1.0 capital; 24 turns cuts debt 2.5 points more.

**Fix.** Charge capital for each turn a measure runs (an upkeep on the policy registry, SE-1), or scale the one-off cost with duration. Cap duration by kind (a speech 1 to 3 turns). Show lifetime cost on the review screen.

### E2 Laws and speeches for a group are a dominant strategy (P1, reproduced)

**Cause.** `policy_events()` (`population/popularity.py`) turns legislate (+1 step) and communicate (+0.4 steps) aimed at a group into approval held for the whole duration, with no fiscal cost and no group that loses. A bot that, on turns 0 to 3, passes a 24-turn law and gives a 24-turn speech for one group each, then does nothing, wins 5/5 offline games at 54.8% and 396 to 430 seats.

**Fix.** Give a group law a losing side (renters' rights cost landlords) and a credibility cost when nothing funds it, let its approval fade unless delivery follows, and price duration (E1). Add this bot and a search-based bot (D4) to `game/balance.py`.

### E3 Aiming at "the country" buys growth that pays for itself (P1, reproduced)

**Cause.** The player's own country accepts tax, spend, deregulate and communicate (`_DOMESTIC` in `policy/feasibility.py`), and `action_factor()` pushes `growth_pct` 2 points per unit directly. Growth then lowers the deficit (weight −0.5) and swells the debt ratio's denominator, against a flat 1.25-point cost. Money aimed at a sector reaches growth at most at 0.5 × 0.9.

**Fix.** Do not let actions push a growth rate. Route national tax and spending through sectors by spending shares, or through a demand node (O3). Until then, allow only communicate on the player's own country.

### E4 Deregulation, appointments and speeches still create free output (P2, reproduced)

**Cause.** SR-12 zeroed legislate's push, but `_ACTION_SIGN` still gives deregulate and appoint +0.5 and communicate +0.2 on any sector or country, with no cost or risk attached. A 0.25-capital speech to the public sector lifts its output by £10bn for as long as it runs.

**Fix.** Give deregulation sector-specific downsides (finance: higher sell-off and capital-flight base rates and a lagged tail risk; energy: price volatility; housing: house prices). Make speeches to a sector move its sentiment for a few turns, not its output.

### E5 A one-turn tax rise pays for a permanent tax cut (P2, reproduced)

**Cause.** `unfunded_tax_cut()` compares costs a turn and ignores how long each measure runs; feasibility's `funded` flag does the same. A 24-turn national tax cut plus a one-turn bank tax rise reads as 0.00 unfunded, yet adds 24.9 step-turns of deficit (cut +30.0, rise −5.1).

**Fix.** Compare each measure's cost over its life (cost × turns paid, or a discounted sum) in `unfunded_tax_cut`, `check_feasibility` and `fit_deficit`.

### E6 Taxing or paying a group has no economic effect (P2, reproduced)

**Cause.** `actions_to_shocks()` sends a group-targeted tax or spend only to the deficit; its incidence is approval alone. Taxing business owners 0.75 and raising pensions 0.75 is deficit-neutral, moves no sector and gains 1.74 points (287 to 320 seats), because pensioners carry 2.5 times business owners' turnout weight.

**Fix.** Map transfers to demand (pensions to consumption across sectors, business taxes to investment in manufacturing and finance) with incidence weights, and add fairness reactions between groups.

## Errors B: engine and game logic

The engine's numbers are now consistent between forecast and resolve; these errors are in rules layered on top of it.

### E7 Institutions revert twice, so whips backfire and rebellions overshoot (P1, reproduced)

**Cause.** The engine fades institution support itself (persistence 0.9), and `_settle_institutions()` in `game/loop.py` then moves `support` 20% back toward home. Settling edits the value but not the engine's carried deviation, so when that deviation fades the engine takes the same ground back a second time.

**Evidence.** With no action Commons support stays near 0.6006. A one-turn whip peaks at 0.68 and falls to 0.589 by turn 12, below never whipping. A 12-turn whip holds only +0.007 by turn 11, then drops to 0.584. A backbench rebellion (−0.05) recovers to 0.6065, above the quiet path.

**Fix.** Keep one reversion. Either settle by adjusting `engine.own` and `engine.levels` for the institution, or drop `_settle_institutions` and model the moving home (start plus half the poll change) as a held push the engine fades toward. Test that a whip never ends below, and a rebellion never above, the quiet path.

### E8 The approval-event cap hides bad news (P2, reproduced)

**Cause.** `target_approval()` sums every active event for a group, then caps the total at ±0.15. Events above the cap act as armour: a later backlash is absorbed until the total falls back under it. With +0.25 of good news, a −0.05 broken-pledge hit leaves pensioners' target at 0.700, unchanged; at +0.15 it falls to 0.650.

**Fix.** Cap gains and losses separately, or cap by source (policies, outcomes, pledges, markets) and sum the capped parts. Test that adding a negative event always lowers the target unless it sits at 0.

### E9 Topping up a wage subsidy costs money and adds no cover (P3, reproduced)

**Cause.** `job_protection()` combines overlapping schemes with `max`, but `fiscal_cost()` charges every new scheme in full. A 0.5 top-up on a running 0.8 scheme costs 0.625 points a turn and leaves cover at 0.80. A related edge: `job_cut()` sizes a cut from this turn's share, so a cut across a scheme that steps down later books the larger saving for every turn.

**Fix.** Charge only the cover added (or add cover, capped at 1), and cost cuts turn by turn against the cover they remove. Both belong in the policy registry (SE-1).

### E10 Pledges on an indicator break in the wrong direction (P2, reproduced)

**Cause.** The interpreter prompt and the `Pledge` docstring define "up" as ruling out a positive magnitude, but `breaks()` in `policy/pledges.py` tests `instrument_sign`. On an indicator the two disagree: a stamp duty rise is magnitude < 0, instrument raise. Written as the prompt asks (tax, down, house prices), "no stamp duty rise" is broken by a stamp duty cut and not by a rise.

**Fix.** Define pledge direction as the tax or spending's own direction everywhere (prompt, docstring, keyword reader), which is what `breaks()` already does, and add a test with an indicator-targeted pledge.

### E11 Acting on a minor in-tray item keeps it alive; ignoring it makes it vanish (P2, reproduced)

**Cause.** In `advance_storylines()`, acting on a side item raises its stage and keeps it open; leaving a minor one (urgency below 0.3) closes it. A pothole row acted on is open at stage 2 four turns later with pressure 0.35; ignored, it closed on turn 0.

**Fix.** Separate progress from severity. Acting should de-escalate (lower pressure, allow a close when the action fits the issue's direction); ignoring should escalate. Judge "acted on" by the forecast effect on the item's nodes, not any action on them, so a cut does not count as help.

### E12 Reversing a measure is penalised as a repeat (P3, reproduced)

**Cause.** The repeat counter in `constrain()` keys on `(kind, target)` and ignores direction. Raising the bank tax 0.6 then cutting it 0.6 next turn applies only −0.3.

**Fix.** Count repeats per direction, and treat an opposite-direction action on a running measure as a U-turn with its own credibility cost (D9) rather than diminishing returns.

### E13 Outcome shocks on a group skip the caps and never leave the engine (P3, reproduced)

**Cause.** `check_candidates()` accepts `new_shocks` on any catalogue id, groups included, and groups keep persistence 1.0. A one-step shock on pensioners lifts approval outside the event cap (0.550 to 0.575 after settling), and the engine still carries the push of 1.0 ten turns later.

**Fix.** Reject `new_shocks` on groups (they belong in `group_effects`), and drop group nodes from the engine's node list.

### E14 Some cast preferences can never be met (P3, reproduced)

**Cause.** `apply_cast()` scores loyalty only on actions aimed directly at a cared-for node. The Chancellor cares about the deficit and the Governor about inflation, which no action may target. A 2.5-point fiscal consolidation (tax rise plus pension cut) leaves the Chancellor at 0.65.

**Fix.** Score loyalty on the forecast's effect on each cared-for node (`response_at`), scaled by size (D5).

## Errors C: offline keyword reading

The negation fixes hold, but substring matching still turns ordinary sentences into the wrong action, including the Budget's own suggested options.

| Player types | Scenario | Read as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spend big to cut hospital waiting lists | any | do nothing |
| Raise taxes to fund public services | Budget | tax rise on the public sector (shrinks it) |
| Cut taxes for business to boost growth | Budget | tax cut on the public sector |
| A steady Budget with no big changes | Budget | speech to the public sector, size 0.9 |
| Announce in Parliament a pay rise for nurses | any | spend on the House of Commons |
| Tighten fire safety rules in housing | any | sacks the Housing Secretary; no action |
| Replace housing benefit with a new rent subsidy | any | sacks the Housing Secretary; no action |
| Ask the Chancellor to replace the energy price cap with a subsidy | any | sacks the Chancellor; no action |

### E15 "Waiting lists" means do nothing (P1, reproduced)

**Cause.** `KeywordInterpreter._one()` tests `nothing|wait|hold firm` as substrings, so "waiting" and "nothing less than" match before any target or verb is read.

**Fix.** Match whole phrases at the start of a clause (`^(do nothing|wait( and see)?|hold firm)\b`).

### E16 The Budget's options are misread (P1, reproduced)

**Cause.** `_TARGETS` has no pattern for business, growth or the economy, so the target falls back to the scenario's first non-indicator node, which on Budget turns is the public sector. Offline play does not show the Budget today (E19), so the Budget rows bite once E19 is fixed; the business fallback misroutes in every scenario now. A single action is then built from the first verb found, so "raise taxes to fund public services" becomes one tax on the sector it meant to fund.

**Fix.** Give calendar options pre-built action packages (the Budget's four options map to known actions) instead of parsing their text. For free text, add targets for business, growth, the economy and households, and split "X to fund Y" into a tax and a spend.

### E17 Mentioning Parliament redirects the policy to the Commons (P2, reproduced)

**Cause.** The legislature pattern is first in `_TARGETS`, so any sentence naming Parliament, MPs or the Commons aims its verb there: "announce in Parliament a pay rise for nurses" spends 0.5 on the Commons (+0.1 support, 0.63 points of deficit). `ENGINE_RULES.md` says such responses are read as a whip, which is true only when no other verb matches.

**Fix.** Treat a venue ("in Parliament", "to the Commons") as `Delivery.venue`, and read the legislature as the target only for whip, backbench or vote language.

### E18 Everyday words sack ministers and drop the policy (P1, reproduced)

**Cause.** `keyword_sackings()` matches `fire|replace|dismiss` anywhere in a clause and the first long word of a minister's role anywhere in it. The clause is then removed from the action text, so the policy itself is lost. Each sacking costs 0.05 Commons support, and the review screen cannot undo a sacking, only `redo`.

**Fix.** Require the verb to govern the person ("sack/fire/replace the Housing Secretary", or the minister's name) within a few words, and let the review screen drop a sacking like an action.

## Errors D: explanations, LLM layer and interface

These do not move the numbers, but they tell the player something false or lose their input.

### E19 Offline play never reaches the calendar, but the dashboard announces it (P2, reproduced)

**Cause.** The CLI's offline source is `ScenarioLibrary`, which has no calendar; only `CannedScenarios` (unused by the CLI) and `LLMScenarioSource` check `calendar_event()`. On turn 3 the dashboard's COMING UP promised the Budget, and the library serves "Housing crunch". The balance and stability harnesses use the library too, so no bot has ever played a Budget. `ENGINE_RULES.md` section 9 says offline uses the event's briefing.

**Fix.** Wrap any `ScenarioSource` in a calendar decorator that returns `scheduled_scenario()` on calendar turns, and use it in the CLI, balance and stability runs.

### E20 "Why" gives the wrong reasons for approval moves (P2, reproduced)

**Cause.** `_approval_reasons()` in `game/explain.py` lists events and `CARES_ABOUT` links only, using the change since last turn. It ignores employment (`EMPLOYS`), the debt penalty, institution influence and voters' baselines. After a public-services spend it says public workers rose 3.2 points because they care about the deficit (+1.85), when the cause is employment in their sector (+0.09 to target). Pensioners' and business owners' falls are mostly the debt penalty, which is never named.

**Fix.** Decompose each group's target into its terms (lean, each cared indicator against baseline, each employing sector, institutions, debt penalty, capped events) and report the largest, as `target_approval()` computes them.

### E21 Measures the model could not map vanish silently (P2, code inspection)

**Cause.** The interpreter returns `unmapped`, but nothing shows it. The CLI asks the interpreter for `dropped()`, which no interpreter defines, so the review screen shows only the mapped actions. The outcome prompt never hears of them either.

**Fix.** Show `unmapped` on the review screen as "Not possible", and pass it to the outcome prompt beside the blocked measures.

### E22 A provider error crashes the turn and loses the confirmed response (P2, code inspection)

**Cause.** `LLMError` subclasses `RuntimeError`. The play loop catches only `ValueError`, `NeedsClarification`, `EOFError` and `KeyboardInterrupt`, so a failed scenario, interpretation or outcome call ends the game with a traceback. A failure in `commit()` comes after the player confirmed, and the proposal is not saved.

**Fix.** Catch `LLMError` at the three call sites, keep the proposal, and offer retry or an offline reading for this turn (SE-12). Save the pending proposal with the pending scenario so `--resume` restores both.

### E23 Stored outcomes are reused whatever the delivery, pledges or blocked measures (P3, code inspection)

**Cause.** `StoredForecaster` matches on action signature and sackings only. A stored narrative written for a consulted, phased package, a broken pledge or a bill the Commons blocked can be replayed for an imposed package that broke nothing and passed.

**Fix.** Add delivery (consulted or not, speed), broken-pledge ids and blocked-measure notes to the match, or regenerate those clauses from the current turn.

### E24 "Stressed" depends on an indicator's units (P3, code inspection)

**Cause.** `summarise_state()` flags a 5% relative move. Unemployment 4.5 to 4.73 is stressed; the energy index 100 to 104 is not; a deficit near 0 is stressed by any move. The flag steers scenario generation.

**Fix.** Flag moves in standard steps (via `scale()`), for example 0.5 steps over the window.

## Reasoning and modelling weaknesses

These are simplifications rather than bugs, but each one rewards a strategy the real world would not, or explains an outcome with the wrong cause.

### M1 Levels and rates are mixed

Sector output is a level, yet it drives UK growth, a rate, through `SUPPLIES` edges; growth then sets unemployment's level. A policy held for 24 turns therefore keeps growth higher for 24 turns, rather than giving a one-off step in output. `gdp_bn` never changes, so the debt ratio's denominator moves with an elevated rate that no output level backs. This is the root of E3.

**Better.** Track output and potential output as levels; derive growth as their change and unemployment from the output gap (Okun's law on the gap).

### M2 The monetary block conflates Bank Rate with gilt yields

The deficit raises Bank Rate directly (weight 0.15), the mini-budget rule moves Bank Rate by 0.4 per step, and the debt reprices toward Bank Rate. The Bank of England is independent in the game's own rules, and in 2022 the shock was in gilt yields. Bank Rate also responds to inflation at 0.5 × 0.9 = 0.45 per point, below one, so real rates fall as inflation rises.

**Better.** Add a gilt yield (Bank Rate path plus a term and risk premium driven by fiscal credibility); reprice debt toward it; set Bank Rate with a rule that answers inflation by more than one for one.

### M3 A trade deal grows the partner's whole economy

A diplomatic action on a trading partner pushes the partner's growth 0.5 points a unit (the EU's, for 12 turns, in the probe), and a speech to a foreign country nudges its growth too. The UK feels it only through the trade edge.

**Better.** Make deals and sanctions move the trade edge weights and sector access (for example EU to UK manufacturing), not the partner's GDP.

### M4 Every sector tax is an output tax, and price caps are free

Taxing a sector removes output, so a windfall tax on energy cuts energy output and raises bills through the −0.6 link. Capping bills (regulate) has no fiscal cost, only a 25% output squeeze on the drivers.

**Better.** Give tax instruments an incidence (profits, consumption, output) and price caps a cost carried by suppliers or the Treasury, chosen by the instrument.

### M5 The electorate is 59% of the population

The four groups' shares sum to 0.59, and vote intention normalises over them. Pensioners hold 38% of the turnout-weighted vote, so pensioner policies dominate (E2, E6).

**Better.** Add the missing groups (private-sector workers, mortgaged homeowners, students) before tuning any group effect.

### M6 The forecast's uncertainty never happens

Resolve always uses the central edge weights. The 10–90% bands drive consistency scoring and narrative, but the world itself is deterministic given the actions and the outcome. Players cannot learn that the economy behaved differently from the forecast, because it never does.

**Better.** See D1: draw a hidden set of weights per game.

### M7 Probabilities still rest on placeholders

Base rates are uncalibrated, consulting anyone lowers strike odds, and the rarest tag bounds a conjunction (SR-15, SR-16 in the backlog, unchanged). They matter more once E2 is fixed, because outcomes become the main source of risk.

## Enhancements: decisioning, forecasting and AI

D1 and D4 give the most for the effort: one makes the forecast's uncertainty real, the other finds the next exploit before a player does.

## Suggested order and tests

Start with the costless levers and the search bot together: the bot fails on main today, and every later balance change can be judged by it.

1. **Close the costless levers.** E1 to E4 with O1 and O2, and D4's search bot plus the four-turn law bot in `game/balance.py`.
2. **Fix the rules layered on the engine.** E7 (one reversion), E8 (split cap), then E5 and E6.
3. **Make offline reading safe.** E15 to E18, the calendar wrapper for E19, then O7's builder.
4. **Make the interface truthful.** E20 to E22, then E23 and E24.
5. **Improve the model.** O3 and O4 for M1 and M2, then D1 so forecasts can be calibrated.

Property tests worth adding, each failing on main today:

- Lifetime capital or deficit cost rises with duration for every action kind.
- No package that costs nothing in deficit, within one turn's capital, beats doing nothing by more than 0.5 vote points at the election.
- Spending aimed at the player's own country never leaves debt lower than doing nothing.
- A whip never leaves Commons support below the quiet path; a rebellion never leaves it above.
- Adding a negative approval event always lowers a group's target unless it is at 0.
- Acting on an in-tray item never keeps it open longer than ignoring it.
- An unfunded tax cut is judged on lifetime cost: a shorter tax rise cannot fund a longer cut.
- The phrases in Errors C map as intended (waiting lists, fire safety, the Budget's options, Parliament as a venue).
